# CAREER EXECUTION ENGINE

**Status:** LOCKED

## Purpose
Convert validated career direction, development evidence, projects, and portfolio proof into a realistic path toward work, clients, users, or an AI-powered business.

## Preconditions

Execution planning requires:
- a primary career direction or explicit target;
- relevant evidence;
- sufficient portfolio/project proof for the chosen execution track.

If evidence is insufficient, do not fabricate readiness. Identify the smallest proof needed.

## Execution Tracks

Canonical tracks:

- Employment
- Freelance
- Contract
- Consulting
- Creator
- AI Service
- AI Product
- AI Business

The agent may recommend one primary track and alternatives when evidence supports them.

## Procedure

1. Inspect primary career direction and Fit, Readiness, Potential.
2. Inspect demonstrated competencies and portfolio evidence.
3. Identify the strongest execution track for the current readiness level.
4. Define positioning.
5. Identify realistic opportunity types.
6. Evaluate opportunities using the canonical opportunity model.
7. Select the smallest useful execution action.
8. Define an observable outcome.
9. Record the result as new evidence when completed.
10. Trigger reassessment when execution feedback materially changes the profile.

## Positioning

Use:

WHO + WHAT + FOR WHOM + OUTCOME

### WHO
The person's relevant role or capability.

### WHAT
What they build, deliver, automate, create, or solve.

### FOR WHOM
The target user, client, team, business, or audience.

### OUTCOME
The meaningful result they aim to produce.

Positioning must be grounded in demonstrated capability. Do not claim expertise that the evidence does not support.

## Opportunity Evaluation

When comparing an opportunity, use:

- Career Alignment — 25%
- Competency Fit — 25%
- Experience/Portfolio — 15%
- Interest — 10%
- Accessibility — 10%
- Growth Potential — 10%
- Compensation — 5%

These weights are decision support, not guarantees of success.

### Interpretation

**Career Alignment:** relevance to the selected career direction.

**Competency Fit:** match between required and demonstrated capabilities.

**Experience/Portfolio:** strength of directly relevant proof.

**Interest:** sustainability based on observed interests and preferences.

**Accessibility:** realistic ability to pursue the opportunity given known constraints.

**Growth Potential:** ability to create stronger future capability, evidence, or opportunities.

**Compensation:** expected economic value when reliable information exists.

Do not rank an opportunity solely by compensation.

## Execution Readiness

Separate:

- career fit;
- readiness for the opportunity;
- potential after development.

A high-fit career with insufficient proof should produce an evidence-building action rather than a false claim of readiness.

## Action Design

Prefer the smallest action that can produce meaningful market or real-world feedback, such as:

- publish a relevant project;
- contact a potential client;
- apply to a targeted role;
- offer a narrowly defined AI service;
- test a workflow with a real user;
- launch a small product experiment;
- obtain feedback on a portfolio case.

Do not prescribe high-volume outreach before positioning and proof are sufficiently clear.

## Evidence and Feedback

Completed execution actions may create:
- experience evidence;
- demonstrated-result evidence;
- user/client feedback;
- portfolio evidence;
- new constraints;
- new opportunity preferences.

These must be fed back into state and may trigger reassessment.

## Reassessment

Re-run execution planning when:
- career direction changes;
- readiness changes materially;
- new portfolio/project evidence appears;
- opportunities or constraints change;
- execution feedback contradicts an assumption.
